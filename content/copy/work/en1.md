## Context
Rising fuel prices were pushing Nigerians toward alternatives, and EN1 wanted to be *the* electric vehicle name in the country. The product was premium, so the digital experience had to feel premium too, while still working for a mobile-first audience on variable connections.

**The core tension:** how do you make something feel luxurious without making it slow, heavy or hard to use?

## My role
As Design Lead, I owned:
- The visual language and the end-to-end UX across all touchpoints
- User flows and prototyping for website, app, configurator, Kitshop and admin
- The design system, its components and its documentation
- Stakeholder workshops aligning product direction with acquisition goals
- Usability validation before each release

## What we learned
Market research and user interviews showed the audience wanted:
1. **Effortless exploration.** Browse and compare without friction.
2. **Local relevance.** Content, pricing and language that feel Nigerian, not imported.
3. **Mobile first.** Most visits would come from phones.

## Key decisions

### 1. Restraint as luxury
**Decision:** A minimal, image-led interface with generous space and subtle motion. The car is the hero, not the UI.
**Trade-off:** Big imagery against mobile performance. We solved it with `[TOSIN: e.g. progressive loading, art-directed crops]`.
`[IMG: website screens; reuse old-site/images/en1-00, en1-01.png]`

### 2. A configurator that builds desire
**Decision:** Real-time colour, trim and feature selection with live pricing, so customers feel ownership before they buy.
`[IMG: configurator, placeholder]`

### 3. One system, five products
**Insight:** Five touchpoints built in parallel would drift apart fast.
**Decision:** Build EN1's first design system early, with tokens, components and patterns shared across web, mobile and admin.
**Result:** 15% faster team velocity and consistent output across platforms.
`[IMG: design system overview, components, placeholder]`

### 4. Internal tools are product too
**Decision:** An admin portal for orders, merch and inquiries, designed to the same standard as the customer-facing products.

## Reflection
This project taught me that in an emerging market, "premium" is about trust and relevance as much as polish. It also taught me that a design system pays off fastest when it starts on day one, not after the third product.
